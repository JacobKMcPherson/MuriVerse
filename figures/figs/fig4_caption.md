**Figure 4. Exploratory case study of MuriVerse predictions for colistin and polymyxin B in mice and rats, compared with intravenous reference measurements.** The post-audit ensemble trained on 178 source-verified intravenous records was applied to colistin A (E1), colistin B (E2), polymyxin B1 and polymyxin B2. Neither drug was identified as a named training record, and no surrogate-sequence query met the specified homology threshold. Because these queries substituted standard residues for Dab, the negative search does not establish chemical unrelatedness. Earlier polymyxin predictions had informed feature-block retention; this application was therefore not an independent external validation. Predictions used intravenous inputs and two alternative N-terminal acyl encodings; the C-terminus was specified as unmodified because the Thr10–Dab4 macrolactam is an internal isopeptide bond rather than C-terminal amidation. Across four congeners and two species, the 16 prediction rows therefore contained 8 distinct values because the alternative N-terminal wording produced identical model inputs. Predictions are drawn in solid species colors (mouse blue, rat green) and intravenous observations in paler tints of the same colors; marker shapes denote congener (B1 circle, B2 triangle, E1 square, E2 diamond). (a) Predicted congener half-lives relative to the intravenous training-label distribution, with the four animal-weighted observed drug–species values shown as dotted lines. Predicted values lie between the mode of the training distribution and its slow shoulder; the two mouse observations lie below the predictions and the two rat observations above them. (b) Predicted half-lives across congeners and species compared with the intravenous animal-weighted observations (grey bars, ±1 SD) and constant baselines derived from the post-audit intravenous training split. Predictions ranged from 0.57 to 0.92 h for observed drug–species values of 0.45–1.90 h. Median absolute fold errors were 2.05 for mouse colistin, 1.26 for mouse polymyxin B, 2.05 for rat colistin and 3.06 for rat polymyxin B, with overprediction in mice and underprediction in rats. A constant was closer than the model in three of the four cells: the species median in both mouse cells and the lipidated intravenous training peptide (a single record) in rat polymyxin B; in rat colistin the model was closer than every constant. Species is a model input, although the rat predictions were only about 15% shorter than the mouse predictions, whereas intravenous half-lives are 3.3- to 3.6-fold longer in rats. (c) Predicted versus observed half-life for each congener in rat and mouse; the dashed line denotes identity. All mouse predictions lie above the identity line and all rat predictions below it. Within this exploratory polymyxin case study, median absolute fold errors across congener predictions were approximately 1.7 in mice and 2.4 in rats; these summaries are distinct from the cross-validated errors in the full intravenous development cohort. E1 and E2 differ by 1.02-fold, so their marks overlap. (d) Study-level intravenous plasma or serum half-lives with the dose (mg/kg) beside each study name and annotated by infection status, measurement context and pharmacokinetic estimation method. Observed values are colored by source (Tam lab, Monash group, other studies); paler symbols denote half-lives derived from published pharmacokinetic parameters rather than directly reported values. Points, lines and bands in the species colors show the shipped-model predictions for each drug and species. Grey bands show the intravenous animal-weighted observed geometric mean ±1 animal-weighted SD on the log scale across dose groups, with dotted grey lines indicating the corresponding summary when each study is counted once. Mean residence times are not included in the half-life comparison. Jin 2009's half-life (0.29 h) is derived from a plotted profile and is approximate, and Liao 2023 is a rat study whose half-life is shorter than its own infusion. Half-life axes are shown in clock time.
